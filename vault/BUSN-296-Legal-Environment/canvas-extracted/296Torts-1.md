Defenses to Negligence
Comparative Negligence – Majority rule in which plaintiff’s share of the fault offsets damages.
Contributory Negligence – Virginia (If plaintiff contributes to his damages, he will be barred from all recovery. Baskett v. Banks, 45 S.E.2d 173) Other contributory jurisdictions include: Alabama, DC, Maryland, NC
Assumption of the Risk
Liebeck v. McDonald’shttps://www.caoc.org/?pg=facts
79 year old sitting in the passenger seat – she was not driving.
Coffee was being held at 185-190 degrees F – 30-40 degrees hotter than coffee made at home.
Third degree burns over 6% of her body.  Medical bills were $10,000.  McDonald’s offered her $800.
McDonald’s knew that customers consumed food and drink in the car. Over 700 people had been severely burned.
Court awarded her $200K in compensatory reduced to $160K and punitive damages of $2.9M (2 days of coffee sales) reduced to $480K.

Malpractice
When a professional has breached his duty of care, the professional may be held liable for a special type of negligence called malpractice.
Almost all states have a Good Samaritan statute that protects medical professionals from civil liability for ordinary negligence when they gratuitously lend aid.  
Intentional Torts
Assault – reasonable apprehension of bodily harm.
Battery – unwanted touching
Garratt v. Dailey https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garratt_v._Dailey
Dailey, a 5 ½ year old, moved a lawn chair that he believed Ruth Garratt was about to sit in.  She suffered severe injuries and sued Dailey for battery. Is he liable for her injuries – is intent present? Yes…if he knew with substantial certainty that she would fall.

Negligent Torts
Burden of proof (B/P) – preponderance of the evidence.  
In order to recover for a tort , there must be…
Act or omission
Duty of Care -- To sue for negligence, there must be proof of a breach of a statutory or common law duty. 
Breach of a ‘duty of care’ and injury
Causation or ‘proximate cause’
Defamation
False untrue statements that hurt someone’s reputation
Slander – oral untrue statements
Libel – written untrue statements
Note: Truth is an absolute defense
References and Recommendations – There is a “qualified privilege” granted to employers who are sued for defamation provided they are acting in good faith. 
Ultra Hazardous Activities
There is absolute liability for engaging in an ultra hazardous activity.
Examples: Blasting with dynamite, keeping wild animals.
Torts
A tort is a violation of a civil duty – it’s a private injury for which a cause of action will lie.
Remedies – money damages or an injunction (on order to refrain from doing an act).
