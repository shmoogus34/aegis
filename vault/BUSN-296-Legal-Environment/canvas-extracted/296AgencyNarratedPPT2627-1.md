Martha refused to pay for them alleging that the manager did not have authority to order blue jeans for the store.  Does Martha have to pay for them? 
Rule: Under agency law, if an agent acts within their apparent authority, the principal is still liable—even if the agent violates secret instructions, unless the third party is aware of the limitation.

Torts
Negligent Torts – are committed when there is a failure to act as a reasonable person would act under the circumstances
Intentional Torts – Battery, assault, defamation (libel and slander)
Respondeat Superior
An employer is liable for the negligent torts of an employee if committed within the scope of the employment
Intentional Torts – an employer is only liable for the intentional torts of an employee is committed within the scope of the employments AND in furtherance of the employer’s business.

Contractual Liability
An agent takes on the contractual capacity of the principal.  Quaere: Can a minor act as an agent?
An agent is not liable on contracts made within the scope of his authority on behalf of a disclosed principal.
An agent is liable on all contracts made on behalf of an undisclosed principal.  
Agency
Agency is based on the express or implied agreement whereby one person (the agent) agrees to act on the behalf of another person (the principal).
When is a principal liable for the actions of an agent?
Fiduciary Duty
A person acting as an agent owes a fiduciary duty to the principal.  In other words, the agent must act in the best interests of the principal.
Ex. Allen Industries v. Sheldon https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/jeffrey-allen-industries-inc-888801237
Joan Kulwin (JK) entered into a contract with Sheldon (S) to sell a building.  If S sold the building by herself, she would receive the entire commission. 
S presented JK with a written offer for $335,000 and JK countered with $350.000.  The contract was signed.
S did not tell JK that there was an oral offer for more than $350,000 because S would have to split the commission with a cooperating broker.
Did S breach her fiduciary duty to JK?  YES!
Termination
Act of the parties.  Notice must be given to those who dealt with or knew of the agency relationship to prevent “apparent authority”
Operation of law – death, insanity, bankruptcy, impossibility.  Notice to third parties is not necessary.
 Agency Formation
Express authority – A principal may grant an agent express authority to act on behalf of the principal.
Statute of Frauds applies (Ex. RE contracts)
A written agency agreement is called a power of attorney. A person acting under a power of attorney is termed an “attorney in fact”.  
Implied authority – comes from actual authority
Apparent authority – The principal represents to a third party that someone is his/her agent.  Ex. Lundberg v. Church Farms case https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/lundberg-v-church-farm-887845300
