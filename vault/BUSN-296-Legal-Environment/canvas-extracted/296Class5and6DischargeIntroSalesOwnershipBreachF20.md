Express Warranties
Include a statement of fact and could arise through a description of goods.
Example:  You buy a ring that is marked 14K gold.  You wear the ring for a couple of weeks and it turns your finger green because it is gold plated brass.  Has the seller breached a warranty?
Potter, v. Ryndall, 22 NC App 129, 205 SE2d 808
Buyer bought an engine to operate his irrigation pump.
Label said it would produce 100HP – buyer needed at least 80 HP.
In actual use, the engine only produced 60HP
Is this a breach of an express warranty?
Implied Warranty of Merchantability
Automatically made by a merchant selling goods that he/she ordinarily sells.
“Good for their ordinary purpose”
Has been applied to the sale of food and drink.  “Naturally present” versus “reasonable expectations” rule.  Consider Goodman v. Wendy’s http://prenhall.com/divisions/bp/app/phblaw/html/cases/cases_html/CASE20_3.html

Implied Warranty of Fitness for a Particular Purpose
Made by any seller (NOT just merchants)
Seller must have reason to know the buyer’s special purpose
Buyer must be relying on seller’s skill and judgment

Black v. Don Schmid Motor Co.
Black bought a 1977 Peugeot that had been driven by a factory rep 22,967 miles for $8,028.85.
The dealer gave Black a 12 month 12,000 mile warranty.
During the first 5 months, Black had to take the car back 5 times for repairs… that were not successful.
Accelerator would stick, transmission leaked, thermostat did not work, AC leaked cold water when he turned corners. 
Black returned the car. 
Holding: A buyer may revoke acceptance within a reasonable amount of time.

Objective Impossibility
Objective impossibility can occur when the subject matter of the contract is destroyed, the contract is now illegal,  or death of a person necessary to complete the contract.
Subjective Impossibility
Subjective impossibility does not destroy a contract (Ex. The cost to perform has increased)  
Ex. Westinghouse yellow cake contracts.
Long-term fixed price contract to sell “yellow cake for $8-10/lb
Market price rose to $30/lb and Westinghouse committed to supply 40,000 tons.

Conditional Contracts
A contract may be subject to a condition precedent or a condition subsequent.
Condition precedent – I will buy your house for $50,000 if I get a satisfactory termite inspection.
Condition subsequent – You can rent my lake house for $800/month unless my lake house sells.
