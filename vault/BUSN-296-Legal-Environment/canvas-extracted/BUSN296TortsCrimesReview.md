Torts
A tort is a violation of a civil duty – it’s a private injury for which a cause of action will lie.
Burden of Proof – a preponderance of the evidence.
Remedies – money damages or an injunction (on order to refrain from doing an act).
Defenses to Negligence
Comparative Negligence – Majority rule in which plaintiff’s share of the fault offsets damages.
Contributory Negligence – Virginia (If plaintiff contributes to his damages, he will be barred from all recovery. Baskett v. Banks, 45 S.E.2d 173) Other contributory jurisdictions include: Alabama, DC, Maryland, NC
Assumption of the Risk
Liebeck v. McDonald’shttps://www.caoc.org/?pg=facts
79 year old sitting in the passenger seat – she was not driving. Coffee was being held at 185-190 degrees F – 30-40 degrees hotter than coffee made at home. Third degree burns over 6% of her body.  Medical bills were $10,000.  McDonald’s offered her $800. McDonald’s knew that customers consumed food and drink in the car. Over 700 people had been severely burned.
Court awarded her $200K in compensatory reduced to $160K and punitive damages of $2.9M (2 days of coffee sales) reduced to $480K.
What result if this case had been tried in a contributory negligence state?
Malpractice
When a professional has breached his duty of care, the professional may be held liable for a special type of negligence called malpractice.
Almost all states have a Good Samaritan statute that protects medical professionals from civil liability for ordinary negligence when they gratuitously lend aid.

Larceny and Shoplifting
Larceny is the taking and carrying away the personal property of another with the intent to permanently deprive the owner.
Hypothetical: Sal went into Wal-Mart and concealed candy bars in the pocket of his coat (to steal them).  When leaving WalMart, Sal was stopped by a security guard and charged with shoplifting. Would he guilty of this crime?
Shoplifting is a statutory form of larceny.  You do not have to leave the store to be convicted of shoplifting.

RICO
If property is used in the commission of a crime OR if property is purchased with money from criminal activities, it may be seized (forfeited) under RICO. $25,000 fine per violation and/or up to 20 years in prison.

Accounting Provisions
Anti-bribery Provisions- Sections 103 and 104 prohibit a company from giving anything of value to a foreign official, political party, or political candidate, to obtain or retain business.  Grease payments are specifically excluded.  In other words, grease payments are legal.

Constitution and Crimes
5th Amendment (Double jeopardy) – you can’t be tried twice for the same crime based on the same set of facts.  
You be tried for a crime and found not guilty and then be sued civilly.
Example: OJ Simpson was found “Not guilty” for the murder of his ex-wife, Nicole Brown Simpson.  Nicole’s parents then sued OJ for Nicole’s wrongful death (for money damages).  Is this allowed under our legal system?  
Fourth Amendment
4th Amendment – Freedom from unreasonable searches and seizures applies to individuals as well as to businesses. 
Note: The 5th Amendment right to refrain from self-incrimination only applies to individuals…NOT to corporations.
Miranda v. Arizona
Although the police don’t have any physical evidence of guilt, John is taken into custody and charged with armed robbery.  John is never read his Miranda rights (i.e. You have the right to remain silent…).  While being interrogated at police headquarters, John makes a full confession.  This is the only evidence of guilt presented at trial.  What is the most likely outcome?
The confession will be excluded from trial.
