Public Use Doctrine
According to the public use doctrine, a patent may not be granted if the invention was used by the public for more than a year prior to filing the application.
Copyright Law
A copyright protects an expression -- a literary or artistic works from being misappropriated.  It also protects computer programs.
Registration is permissive.
Exclusive right to use, reproduce, publicly perform or display.
Individual’s copyright lasts for the life of the creator plus 70 years.  The Mickey Mouse Copyright Term Extension Act added 20 years to copyrights; thus we have the somewhat odd number.   
Fair Use Doctrine (17 U.S. Code 107)
The fair use doctrine permits certain limited unauthorized use of copyrighted materials.  The following four factors are considered:
Purpose (commercial vs. educational)
Nature of the copyrighted work
Amount used
Effect on the market – was the value of the copyrighted work harmed?
We will consider two cases – “Professor Publishing” and Kinko’s and the other involves a parody of “The Pretty Woman” song.
Basic Books v. Kinko’s
Kinko’s was photocopying copyrighted works to create “coursepacks”. Was this “fair use” for  educational purposes? NO!  Court ordered Kinko’s to stop and awarded the plaintiffs court costs, damages and attorney’s fees in excess of $2M!
