Sales (Art 2 of the UCC)
Bilateral or Unilateral?
A contract is either a unilateral contract or a bilateral contract. A bilateral contract is where a promise is exchanged for another promise.
A unilateral offer is accepted by performing an act.  (Ex. Reward) To accept the unilateral offer, you must be aware that it exists. 
Types of Contracts
Express or Implied? 
Express – a contract formed through language (either written or oral).  
Implied – the terms are implied from the conduct of the parties.  For example, going to the doctor.
What if Consideration is NOT Present?
Consideration is not required if the document is a “formal contract” or for an agreement/promise to donate money to a charity.
“Merchant’s firm offer” (Art 2 Sales) does not have to be supported by consideration – It must be in writing, signed, and  is irrevocable for the period of time stated in the offer (up to 3 months). 
A promise that that is not supported by consideration may be enforced by a court under “promissory estoppel” – the promisor has reason to foresee detrimental reliance on the part of the promise; and the promise relies to their detriment.  
Elements of Legally Enforceable Contracts
Offer – expresses a willingness to enter into a contract
Acceptance – mirrors the offer
Consideration – the price paid for a promise. NOTE” Forbearance (giving up a right to do something) could be consideration. 
Competent Parties – mentally competent and over 18
Lawful Objective  -- illegal contracts are unenforceable
Proper Form and the Statute of Frauds – Certain contracts (NOT ALL) must be in writing and signed by the person against whom enforcement is sought.
Court System
In Virginia, the General District Court is “small claims” court. Many people represent themselves in this court. 
A court ruling becomes precedent to be followed in later cases is termed “stare decisis” (to stand by the decision).
A dispute may be heard/tried in federal court if:
The U.S. is a party
The cause of action involved a federal law, the US Constitution, or a U.S. treaty.
Or if there is “diversity” – a case between citizens of different states in which at least $75,000 is in controversy.

Warranties (Art. 2)
Express warranty may be a statement of fact. Note: “This is a great car” is NOT a statement of fact. 
Implied Warranty of Merchantability is made by merchant sellers and are disclaimed when:
Goods are sold “as is” or “with all faults” and/or when the merchant seller uses a conspicuous disclaimer that uses the term “merchantability”. 
Implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose
Applies to all sellers (not just merchants).
Buyer relies on the seller’s skill and judgment to pick out goods to meet the stated needs of the buyer.
Know These Terms
A contract in which both parties have performed is called an executed contract. 
Counteroffer– when an acceptance adds an additional term, it is a counteroffer and a counteroffer revokes the offer.  
Scienter – is intent to deceive and is an element of fraud. 
Minors 
A minor may avoid a contract at any time before turning 18.
Minors must pay for “necessaries” (food, clothing, shelter)
By statute in most states, a minor may be liable for contracts for medical care, education loans, and contracts made while running a business.

Remedies/Damages
Specific Performance is not always available as a remedy. It may be granted if the contract is for the sale of land or an item that is considered to be unique (i.e. and antique, work of art, or something of special sentimental value). 
“Liquidated damages” – Parties to a contract agree in advance what the damages will be if there is a breach of contract. 
Three types of monetary damages are:
Nominal damages -- $1.00
Punitive damages
Compensatory damages
Basic Contract Knowledge
Illegal contracts are VOID and unenforceable. The court leaves the parties where it finds them. 
Ambiguous contract terms are strictly construed (interpreted) against the person who wrote the contract.
Invitation to negotiate (i.e. ad in a newspaper) is not an offer.
A contract made under extreme duress is VOID. For example --“Sign this contract or I’ll shoot you.” 
Revocation
Incapacity of the offeror or offeree before acceptance
Lapse of time
Destruction of the subject matter specified in the contract.
NOTE: Once a person has been determined to be incompetent (by a court having proper jurisdiction), that person no longer has the ability to enter into valid contracts – they are void.
MISTAKES
 A unilateral mistake generally has no effect on the validity of a contract unless it is so big that the other contracting party knew or should have known that a mistake was being made. 
A mutual mistake of fact (Ex. Treasure Salvors case and the Atocha) makes a contract VOID. 
Statute of Frauds
The Statute of Frauds requires that certain contracts (NOT ALL) must be in writing. 
The following contracts should be in writing:
Pre-nuptial agreements
Contracts to buy/sell real property (land)
Contracts that can’t be performed in a year (i.e. two-year  employment contract).
Contracts for the sale of goods costing $500 or more (Art 2 Sales).
Exception: Specially manufactured goods not suitable for resale in the ordinary course of business (Ex. Flowers Bakery v. RP Packaging).
Quasi Contract
Applied to avoid UNJUST ENRICHMENT. 
Hypothetical: Suppose your neighbor enters into a contract to have her house painted while she is away on vacation.  By mistake the house painter shows up to your house.  You are aware of that he is painting your house and you do not stop him.  Do you owe the house painter? YES. What would happen if you don’t realize that he is painting your house, and he paints it pink…your least favorite color? He would have to repaint your house at his expense.
