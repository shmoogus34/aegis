Partnerships
Creation – a written partnership is not required
Partnership by estoppel (by default)
Statute of Frauds – Can the partnership be completed in a year?
Characteristics
Co-ownership (2 or more)
Business as co-owners for profit
Sharing profits/losses (NOT gross receipts)
Shared control
Next class we will discuss the disadvantage of  unlimited personal liability.
Allocation of Profit and Loss
Partners share equally in the profits and losses unless otherwise provided in the partnership agreement.
If the partnership provides for the sharing of profits but is silent on the sharing of losses, then losses are split in the same proportion as profits.
Ex.  Liz and Blake are partners.  Liz contributes $80K and Blake contributes $20K to start the business.  They do NOT have a written partnership agreement.  In the first year, they have profits of $100K.  How will they split the profits?
Corporations
Creature of government grant  -- you must comply with the state statute
Perpetual existence – The corporation continues even if one of the shareholders dies.
Limited Liability – “corporate shield” from personal liability.
Disadvantages:
Double taxation of profits paid out as dividends
Following corporate formalities
Creation of a C Corp
Articles of Incorporation -- filed with the Secretary of State.
RMBCA 2.02(a) – Name, registered agent, # of shares that may be issued, names and addresses of initial incorporators. 
Corporate Structure
Shareholders -- Elect the board of directors.  Shareholders have the  right to examine corporate books.
Board of Directors – appoints the officers and may declare a dividend.
Officers -- oversee day-to-day operations.
Limited Partnerships
Limited partner’s liability is limited to his/her capital contribution.
Created under state law.  You must comply with the statute.  Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act (RULPA)
Must have at least one general partner and one or more limited partners
Limited partner’s name may not appear in the firm name
Certificate of Limited Partnership must be filed.
Limited Partnerships: Sharing Profits and Losses
Profits and losses are shared as per the articles of agreement.  
If the agreement is silent on sharing profits and losses, then they are shared in accordance with the proportionate share of capital contributions.  
THIS DIFFERS FROM A GENERAL PARTNERSHIP!

Limited Liability Corporation

Joint Venture
Single business venture – the association of 2 or more people to carry out a single transaction for profit.  Example:  Three corporations and two individuals pool their equipment and expertise to construct a tunnel.  
Liability – Joint venturers have unlimited liability and are liable for the negligence of their co-venturers.
Unincorporated Associations 
No legal existence and cannot sue or be sued.
Member may be held liable if he/she participated or ratified the act of the unincorporated association.  
Franchise
Method of doing business
The franchisor as the holder of a trademark, tradename or copyright, licenses others under certain conditions to use the tradename, trademark or copyright, in purveying goods or services.   
Liability to third parties – as a rule, the franchisor is not liable for the torts or contracts entered into by the franchisee.
