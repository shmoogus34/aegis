Losing Perfected S.I.
A perfected security interest may be lost.
Resale of a consumer good without notice of the security interest (see example on next slide). 
With a possessory security interest, perfection  is lost if the secured party voluntarily surrenders possession of the collateral.  
Removal to another state – The secured party has a four-month grace period to perfect in the second state.  
In Re Scott (KY, 1985)
7/2/1980 Scott executed a security agreement giving National Bank a security interest in a 1980 Marquis Mercruiser boat.
7/14/1980 – Bank filed a financing statement where Scott lived.
May 1981, Scott sold the boat to his neighbor, Bass – Bass did not know about the security interest.
Scott defaulted on the loan and the Bank repossessed the boat.  Who wins?

Secured Transactions
Revised Article 9 UCC
A secured transaction is created by giving the creditor a security interest in the personal property of the debtor.  
Two steps:
Attachment (3 requirements)
Perfection (3 methods)
Consumer Good Resale
Rawlings bought a laptop from A-1 Connections to use at home.
Rawlings made a $300 down payment and agreed to make monthly payments on the balance.
A security agreement was signed and a financing statement was never filed.
When Rawlings still owed $800 on the laptop, he sold it to his neighbor, Jones for $800 (cash).
Rawlings defaulted on the loan to A-1.  Now A-1 seeks to repossess the laptop from Jones.  Will A-1 be successful? 
Disposing of the Collateral
Secured party may keep the collateral.
Notice must be given and the debtor has 21 days to object.
Exception: Consumer goods and debtor has paid at least 60%.  If this is the case, the secured party must sell the consumer goods within 90 days after repossession.  The creditor may not retain the goods unless the debtor waives the sale after default.    
Termination Statement (UCC-3)
Must be filed within 10 days of the debtor making a proper demand.
If the collateral is consumer goods, the termination statement must be filed within 30 days…no demand is necessary.

Applying the Proceeds
Secured party’s reasonable expenses
Debt secured by the collateral is paid
Secured debts of subordinate parties are paid
What if there is excess?  It is returned to the debtor.  
What about an unpaid balance?  The debtor still owes it.  
Method of Selling the Collateral
The debtor must be given notice that the goods are being sold UNLESS the goods are perishable or are threatening to rapidly decline in value or they are sold on a recognized market.
Secured party must act in a commercially reasonable manner.
Public sale – purchaser takes free of any right the debtor had in the collateral.
Private sale – in a good faith sale, the buyer takes free of the debtor’s rights in the collateral
Rights of a Secured Party on Default of the Debtor
Secured party may obtain the collateral through judicial process.  
Take the debtor to court
Prove the security interest
Have the sheriff repossess the collateral
Self-help repossession is available provided it can be done without a breach of the peace.
Right of Redemption -- The debtor always has the right to redeem the collateral before it is sold.
