Agency
Agency is based on the express or implied agreement whereby one person (the agent) agrees to act on the behalf of another person (the principal).
Question: When is a principal liable for the actions of an agent?
Termination
Act of the parties.  Notice must be given to those who dealt with or knew of the agency relationship to prevent “apparent authority”
Operation of law – death, insanity, bankruptcy, impossibility.  Notice to third parties is not necessary.
Lundberg v. Church Farm
Gilbert Church, the owner of Church Farms, Inc., advertised its well-bred stallion Imperial Guard for breeding rights at $50,000, directing all inquiries to “Herb Bagley, Manager.” 
Herb Bagley lived at Church Farms and was the only person available to visitors. 
Vern Lundberg went to Church Farms and Bagley stated that Imperial Guard would remain in Illinois for at least a two-year period.
Lundberg and Bagley executed a two-year breeding rights contract. 
When Gil Church moved Imperial Guard to Oklahoma prior to the second year of the contract, Lundberg brought suit for breach of contract. 
Church testified that Bagley had no authority to sign contracts for Church Farms. Decide. [Lundberg v. Church Farms, Inc., 502 N.E.2d 806 (Ill.)]
Authority
Express authority
Implied authority – comes from actual authority
Apparent authority – The principal represents to a third party that someone is his/her agent. Consider Lundberg v. Church Farm https://casetext.com/case/lundberg-v-church-farm-inc (next slide)
Fiduciary Duty
A person acting as an agent owes a fiduciary duty to the principal.  In other words, the agent must act in the best interests of the principal.
Ex. Allen Industries v. Sheldon
K entered into a contract with S to sell a building.  If S sold the building by herself, she would receive the entire commission. 
S presented K with a written offer for $335,000 and K countered with $350.000.  The contract was signed.
S did not tell K that there was an oral offer for more tha $350,000 because S would have to split the commission with a cooperating broker.
Did S breach her fiduciary duty?  YES
Torts
Negligent Torts – are committed when there is a failure to act as a reasonable person would act under the circumstances
Intentional Torts – Battery, assault, defamation (libel and slander)
Statute of Frauds

Edgewater v. Gadzke and Walgreen
Gatzke (defendant) worked for Walgreen (defendant) supervising the opening of a new store. 
Gatzke stayed at a motel owned by Edgewater (plaintiff). Gatzke was on call 24 hours per day and considered the motel room his “office away from home.” 
After consuming alcohol and eating out, Gatzke returned to his motel room and smoked a cigarette while filling out an expense report for Walgreen. 
Gatzke’s cigarette started a fire in the room, causing serious damage to the motel.

Respondeat Superior
An employer is liable for the negligent torts of an employee if committed within the scope of the employment
Intentional Torts – an employer is only liable for the intentional torts of an employee is committed within the scope of the employments AND in furtherance of the employer’s business.
Contractual Liability
An agent takes on the contractual capacity of the principal.  Quaere: Can a minor act as an agent?
An agent is not liable on contracts made within the scope of his authority on behalf of a disclosed principal.
An agent is liable on all contracts made on behalf of an undisclosed principal.
