Liability of Partners
 Under the Revised Uniform Partnership Act (RUPA), partners are jointly and severally liable for all debts -- torts and contracts.
The injured party may sue each partner individually or together.

Dissolution
Ends the right of the partnership to exist as a going concern.
It may occur by automatically…in other words by operation of law (death, bankruptcy, mental incapacity) OR
By act of the parties -- agreement, addition or withdrawal of one or more of the partners.
NOTE:  When dissolution is caused by act of the partners, constructive notice must be given to those who knew of the partnership’s existence and actual notice must be given to third parties who actually dealt with the partnership.  If notice is not given, then the partnership (and the former partners) may have continuing liability for contracts that are subsequently entered into by former partners.

Limited Partnerships
State statutes (RULPA)
At least one general partner and one limited partner
Limited partner is liable up to capital contribution.
Limited partner has restriction on active participation in partnership and name must not appear in the partnership name.
A limited partner is not in a fiduciary relationship and may own a competing business,
Limited partner may inspect books

Joint Venture
 Association of 2 or more people to carry out a single transaction for profit.
Joint venturers have unlimited liability and are liable for the negligence of co-venturers.
A joint venture is treated as a partnership for tax purposes.  Profits are taxed when earned.

Incorporation Process
First step – Articles of Incorporation filed with SCC http://www.scc.virginia.gov/clk/dom_corp.aspx They include name, duration, purpose, names and addresses of the incorporators, capital structure and the name of the registered agent.  
The purpose limits the contractual capacity of the corporation. Virginia does not require you to state a corporate purpose in the articles of incorporation. All Virginia corporations are formed for the purpose of engaging in any lawful business.
Ultra vires – a contract that exceeds the purpose.  If it is executory, it is unenforceable.

Nature of a Corporation
A corporation is an artificial person -- Separate and distinct  from shareholders. Characteristics:
Limited liability
Ease of transferability of ownership interest
Perpetual existence
Centralized management – the shareholders are the owners and elect the board of directors.  The board of directors choose the officers.
Methods of Electing the Board
Proxy – shareholders do not have to be present to vote.  
Voting methods
Straight voting – Each shareholder votes the number of shares that he/she owns for each open position.
Cumulative voting – A shareholder may place all of his votes for one position by multiplying the number of shares owned by the number of directors being elected.
