Capacity
Mental Incapacity
Can a person who has been legally adjudicated incompetent enter into a valid contract? NO.
 Once a person has been declared by a court to be incompetent, any agreements subsequently entered into are void.   
Is it possible for a person who is mentally ill to have a “lucid interval”? YES.
Intoxication – contracts may be “voidable”.  Test is whether a person knew what they were doing.
Corporations – When a contract is beyond the corporate purpose, it is said to be “ultra vires” or unenforceable.  As a practical matter, most corporate charters have a very broad corporate purpose (all legal business) and thus, this is rare.
Intoxication
Just because a person is intoxicated, doesn’t mean that a contract will be voided.
For intoxication to affect the validity of a contract, a person must be unaware of the legal consequences of their actions.  
 Remember Lucy v. Zehmer – 
VA Sup Ct held that Zehmer was not intoxicated to the point of being unable to comprehend the nature and consequences of the instrument he executed.   
Unilateral Mistake
Unilateral Mistake – generally does not affect the validity of a contract…unless it is so big that the other party knew or should have known.
Example:  You and I orally agree that I will sell you my Smart car for $2,000.  I am the world’s worst key boarder and I make an error.  The written contract says that I agree to sell you my Smart car for $200…not $2,000!  You knew or should have known that I made an error so we do not have a contract.
Mutual Mistake
A mutual mistake of law 
(Ex. zoning ordinance) does not affect the validity of a contract.
A mutual mistake of fact voids a contract 
(Ex. Florida v. Treasure Salvors) http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hIe8xDnveK4
Valid Agreements
Mutual Assent =  Offer + Acceptance
Offer 
Communication is mandatory.  If the offer is not communicated from the offeror to the offeree, then the offer may not be accepted.
Offer made in jest (as a joke) is not valid.
Invitation to negotiate is NOT an offer – Ex. Ad in the newspaper or a sales circular. (handouts p. 17)
Other Defenses
Fraud in the inducement is a misrepresentation of a material fact that is relied upon to the detriment of the relying party.
The agreement is voidable and money damages may be sought.
There must be scienter (intent to deceive). And the misrepresentation must be a statement of fact.
Fraud in the execution creates a void agreement.  This occurs in the form of the agreement.  
Mental Capacity
Lucid interval – It is possible for someone who is mentally ill to have a lucid interval during which they have capacity to contract.  
Once someone is declared incompetent by a court, they no longer have capacity to contract.  
Duress
Duress is a wrongful threat. The threat is viewed through the eyes of the threatened party.  
Ordinary Duress – agreement is voidable.  
Extreme duress – voids an agreement
Consideration
Consideration is the price paid for a promise.
Consideration could be money, services, goods, or giving up a right to do something (forbearance).    
There must be a new legal detriment…something you are not already obligated to do.

CAPACITY -- MINORS
Minors lack capacity to contract; thus, their contracts are voidable at their election
What if a minor misrepresents his age?
General Rule: Minors must pay for the reasonable value of “necessaries” (food, clothing, shelter).
By statute in most states, minors may not avoid education loans, medical bills, and contracts made while running a business
Mailbox Rule
An acceptance is effective when it is mailed.
Exception:
Offer states that the acceptance is not effective until it is received. 
Revocation
Offer may be revoked prior to acceptance.  Exception: Option contract/option offer – a binding contract to keep an offer open.

Acceptance
An effective acceptance must be unequivocal.  It must “mirror” the offer.
If acceptance adds a new term, it is a “counteroffer”.
A counteroffer, revokes an offer.
Example:  I offer to sell my house to you for $50,000.  You decline my offer and offer to buy my house for $45,000.
Note: A mere inquiry is not a counteroffer.  Would you be willing to accept less than $50,000 for your house?
