Consideration
Price paid for a promise.
What if it is missing?  The promise is not binding.
Courts do not judge the adequacy of consideration.
Forbearance as consideration.  Hamer v. Sidway
Contracts that Must be in Writing
Contracts not to be performed within a year
Agreement to sell real property – Exception: Purchaser goes into possession and either makes a down payment or substantial improvements. 
Promise to pay the debt of another – Exception: Main purpose doctrine
Contract of an executor to pay the debt of a decedent
Promise in consideration of marriage (Pre-nuptial agreement)
Contract for the sale of goods costing $500 or more. – Exception: Specially manufactured goods 
Statute of Frauds
“No action shall be brought in the following types of contracts unless the agreement or some memorandum or note thereof shall be in writing and signed by the party to be charged.”
Certain Contracts (not all) must be in writing to be enforceable. 
Must be signed by the party being sued.
The writing must identify the contracting parties and specify the essential terms.  
Effect of non-compliance is that the oral promise is NOT enforceable.
Auctions
The auctioneer’s statements are merely an invitation to bid.
Each bid is an offer.
Acceptance occurs with the fall of the auctioneer’s hammer.
Once a bid has been accepted, the auctioneer cannot legally cancel the sale.
“With reserve” – the item does not have to be sold to the highest bidder.
“Without reserve” – the item must be sold to the highest bidder.

Formal Contracts/Sealed Instrument
Promissory estoppel 
Promisor has reason to foresee detrimental reliance
Promisee did in fact rely on the promise.
Promisee would sustain a substantial loss if the promise were not performed.
NOTE:  Many courts enforce promises not supported by consideration by applying the doctrine of promissory estoppel.  Essentially the court ignores the absence of consideration in order to attain a just result.  
What is not Consideration
Promise to perform an existing obligation (Ex. construction contract)
Past Consideration – At graduation, you are so grateful that you promise to pay your Aunt Stella $10,000 for allowing you to live with her all four years that you spent at Longwood.
Consideration
Consideration is the price paid for a promise.
Consideration could be money, services, goods, or giving up a right to do something (forbearance).  
It is the element that distinguishes a gift from a binding contract.
Forbearance or giving up a right to do something could be “consideration”.  Would you give up your Smart phone for a year for $100,000?  https://www.vitaminwater.com/legal/scrollfree_rules/?_ga=2.128598092.154181522.1545059548-1324931287.1545059548
 Note: To constitute consideration, there must be a new legal detriment.  In other words…something you are not already obligated to do.
PAROL EVIDENCE RULE
When a contract is in writing, the general rule is that “parol evidence” (or extrinsic evidence that is outside the contract) may not contradict or modify the terms of the written agreement.  If a contract is ambiguous, then a court may allow parole evidence.  
Ex. I have 2 Smart cars and I agree to sell you my Smart car for $2,500.

Regulation of Business
Licensing – Laws may require that a person obtain a license, certificate or diploma before operating a business.  If this requirement is to protect the public (Ex. A lawyer passing the bar exam), a contract made by an unlicensed person is unenforceable.  An unlicensed person may collect on a contract if the license was required only to raise money for a locality (Ex. Farmville business license). 
Agreements not to compete are often included in employment contracts or with the sale of a business.  They are enforced if they are not overly broad in scope or duration.

Perkins v. Standard Oil
Perkins was obligated to make substantial investments in storage facilities, delivery trucks and other equipment. 
Perkins was only allowed to sell Standards products and he must sell a minimum quantity.   
Plaintiff's compensation was based exclusively on the sales he made to his customers. 
Standard sold products directly to Perkins customers. 
Perkins asked the court to imply a protective term that prohibited Standard from taking Perkins customers.
Protective term was implied.
