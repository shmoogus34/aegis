PAROL EVIDENCE RULE
When a contract is in writing, the general rule is that “parol evidence” (spoken words) will not be allowed to contradict or modify the terms of the written agreement. If a contract is ambiguous, then a court may allow parol evidence.  
Ex. I have 3 Smart cars and I agree to sell you my Smart car for $2,500.  
Effect of Illegality
Illegal agreement is void and courts will not aid either party (Ex. Gambling debt).
Good faith and fairness –   When there is grossly disproportionate bargaining power, the courts may come to the aid of the weaker party.

Perkins v. Standard Oil
Perkins was obligated to make substantial investments in storage facilities, delivery trucks and other equipment. 
Perkins was only allowed to sell Standards products and he must sell a minimum quantity.   
Plaintiff's compensation was based exclusively on the sales he made to his customers. 
Standard sold products directly to Perkins customers. 
Perkins asked the court to imply a protective term that prohibited Standard from taking Perkins customers.
Protective term was implied.  
Bovard v. American Horse Enterprises
Defendant purchased a company from plaintiff and signed some promissory notes payable to the plaintiff.  
Plaintiff sued to enforce the notes.
Held: Court refused to enforce this contract for “public policy reasons”.

Statute of Frauds
“No action shall be brought in the following types of contracts unless the agreement or some memorandum or note thereof shall be in writing and signed by the party to be charged.”
Certain Contracts (not all) must be in writing to be enforceable. 
Must be signed by the party being sued.
The writing must identify the contracting parties and specify the essential terms.  
Effect of non-compliance is that the oral promise is NOT enforceable.
AUCTIONS
The auctioneer’s statements are merely an invitation to bid.
Each bid is an offer.
Acceptance occurs with the fall of the auctioneer’s hammer.
Once a bid has been accepted, the auctioneer cannot legally cancel the sale.
“With reserve” – the item does not have to be sold to the highest bidder.
“Without reserve” – the item must be sold to the highest bidder.
Consideration
Consideration is the price paid for a promise.
Consideration could be money, services, goods, or giving up a right to do something (forbearance).  
It is the element that distinguishes a gift from a binding contract.
Forbearance or giving up a right to do something could be “consideration”.  Would you give up your Smart phone for a year for $100,000?  https://www.vitaminwater.com/legal/scrollfree_rules/?_ga=2.128598092.154181522.1545059548-1324931287.1545059548
 Note: To constitute consideration, there must be a new legal detriment.  In other words…something you are not already obligated to do.

Promissory estoppel
