## SECTION 1: American Legal System

### Multiple Choice Traps
- **Federal question jurisdiction** ≠ diversity jurisdiction — know the difference
- **Administrative law** comes from agencies (OSHA, EPA, FTC), not Congress directly
- **Stare decisis** = courts follow precedent ("to stand by what has been decided")
- **Holding** = the legal rule from a case; **dicta** = non-binding commentary

### Key Case Concepts
- **Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz**: Personal jurisdiction via minimum contacts
- **Erie Doctrine**: Federal courts apply state substantive law in diversity cases

## SECTION 2: Torts — Master List

### Negligence Framework (MEMORIZE)
```
Duty → Breach → Causation (actual + proximate) → Damages
```
- Failing ANY element = no negligence claim
- "Reasonable person" standard for duty/breach
- Forseeability test for proximate cause

### Intentional Torts Quick Reference
| Tort | Key Element | Business Context |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| Battery | Harmful/offensive contact, NO consent | Assault in workplace |
| Assault | Imminent apprehension | Threatening employee |
| False Imprisonment | Confinement, no exit | Bad shoplifter detention |
| Defamation | FALSE statement | Bad Glassdoor review |
| Fraud | Knowingly false + reliance | Deceptive contract |

### Products Liability — 3 Types
1. **Manufacturing defect**: One product is wrong (came out of factory broken)
2. **Design defect**: ALL products in line are dangerous
3. **Failure to warn**: Product is dangerous without proper warning

### Strict Liability Triggers
- Abnormally dangerous activities (explosives, toxic chemicals)
- Keeping wild/exotic animals
- Defective products (strict products liability)

## SECTION 3: Contracts

### Offer + Acceptance Analysis Steps
1. Was there a **valid offer**? (definite terms, communicated, intent)
2. Was the offer still **open**? (not revoked, rejected, lapsed, counter-offered)
3. Was there a valid **acceptance**? (mirror image? mailbox rule?)
4. Was there **consideration**? (bargained-for exchange, not a gift)
5. Any **defenses**? (fraud, duress, incapacity, illegality, mistake)

### Mailbox Rule
- Acceptance is effective when **dispatched** (dropped in mailbox)
- Revocation only effective when **received**
- Exception: If offer says "acceptance effective upon receipt" → receipt rule applies

### Common Exam Scenarios
**Scenario**: A promises to pay B $500 for B's old laptop. B says "Sure, but I want $600."
→ B's response is a **counter-offer** (not an acceptance) — original offer terminated.

**Scenario**: Acme Corp hires Jones as sales manager. Jones embezzles. Is Acme liable?
→ **Agency law**: Acme may be liable under **respondeat superior** if Jones acted in scope of employment.

**Scenario**: Contract for 500 widgets is silent on delivery date. What applies?
→ **UCC Article 2** (goods). Delivery within a **reasonable time**.
