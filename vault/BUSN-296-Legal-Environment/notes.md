# BUSN-296 Lecture Notes

## Unit 1: The American Legal System

### Sources of Law (Hierarchy)
1. **U.S. Constitution** (supreme)
2. **Federal Statutes** (acts of Congress)
3. **Federal Administrative Law** (agency regulations: FTC, SEC, OSHA)
4. **State Constitutions**
5. **State Statutes**
6. **State Common Law** (case law / judge-made law)
7. **Local Ordinances**

### Court System
**Federal Courts:**
- U.S. District Courts → U.S. Courts of Appeals (circuits) → U.S. Supreme Court
- Jurisdiction: Federal question, diversity (>$75k, different states)

**State Courts:**
- Trial Court → Appellate Court → State Supreme Court
- General jurisdiction: most business disputes

### Constitutional Protections for Business
- **Commerce Clause** (Art. I §8): Congress regulates interstate commerce
- **Due Process** (5th, 14th Amend.): Substantive + Procedural
- **Equal Protection** (14th Amend.): No arbitrary discrimination
- **Contracts Clause**: States cannot impair existing contracts
- **Takings Clause** (5th Amend.): Just compensation for eminent domain
- **1st Amendment**: Commercial speech protections (limited)

### Dispute Resolution
- **Litigation**: formal court process
- **Arbitration**: binding/non-binding, ADR
- **Mediation**: facilitated negotiation, non-binding
- **Negotiation**: direct party resolution

## Unit 2: Tort Law

### What is a Tort?
A civil wrong (not a crime) that causes harm to another, giving rise to a lawsuit for damages.

### Negligence (Most Common Business Tort)
**4 Elements — ALL required:**
1. **Duty of Care**: Legal obligation to act reasonably
2. **Breach**: Failure to meet the standard of care
3. **Causation**: Breach caused the harm
   - **Actual cause** ("but for"): But for the breach, harm wouldn't occur
   - **Proximate cause**: Foreseeable harm, no superseding cause
4. **Damages**: Actual harm suffered

**Defenses to Negligence:**
- **Contributory Negligence**: Plaintiff's own fault bars recovery (harsh)
- **Comparative Negligence**: Fault apportioned by percentage (most states)
- **Assumption of Risk**: Plaintiff knowingly accepted the risk

### Intentional Torts
| Tort | Elements | Example |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Battery** | Intentional harmful/offensive contact | Punch in boardroom |
| **Assault** | Intentional act causing apprehension of battery | Threatening gesture |
| **False Imprisonment** | Intentional confinement without consent/authority | Detaining suspected shoplifter improperly |
| **Defamation** | False statement of fact, published, causing harm | False bad review |
| **Fraud** | Misrepresentation of material fact, reliance, damages | Fraudulent sales pitch |
| **Trespass** | Unauthorized entry onto land or chattel | Dumping on property |
| **IIED** | Extreme/outrageous conduct causing severe emotional distress | Harassment |

**Defamation specifics:**
- **Libel**: Written/recorded
- **Slander**: Spoken
- Public figures must prove **actual malice** (NYT v. Sullivan)
- Truth is an absolute defense

### Strict Liability
- Liability without fault — used for:
  - **Abnormally dangerous activities**: blasting, keeping wild animals
  - **Products liability**: defective products causing harm
  - **Workers' compensation**: employer pays regardless of fault

### Products Liability
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Manufacturing defect** | Product deviated from design during production |
| **Design defect** | Entire product line is unreasonably dangerous |
| **Failure to warn** | Inadequate instructions or warnings |

## Unit 3: Contract Law

### Contract Definition
A **legally enforceable agreement** between two or more parties with mutual obligations.

### 6 Elements of a Valid Contract
1. **Offer**: Definite, communicated to offeree, intent to be bound
2. **Acceptance**: Mirror image rule (common law) / UCC is more flexible
3. **Consideration**: Bargained-for exchange (money, goods, services, forbearance)
4. **Capacity**: Legal ability to contract (18+, not mentally incapacitated, not intoxicated)
5. **Legality**: Subject matter must be legal
6. **Mutual Assent**: Meeting of the minds — no fraud, duress, undue influence, mistake

### Offer Termination
- **Revocation**: Offeror withdraws before acceptance
- **Rejection**: Offeree says no
- **Counter-offer**: New terms = rejection + new offer
- **Lapse of time**: Expires per terms or reasonable time
- **Death/incapacity**: Terminates offer

### Contract Performance & Breach
**Types of Breach:**
- **Material breach**: significant failure, other party discharged from duties
- **Minor breach**: substantial performance with slight deviation, remedy is damages
- **Anticipatory repudiation**: party indicates in advance they won't perform

**Remedies for Breach:**
| Remedy | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Compensatory** | Direct losses from breach |
| **Consequential** | Foreseeable indirect losses |
| **Punitive** | Rare in contracts; available for fraud |
| **Nominal** | Breach proven but no real damages |
| **Specific Performance** | Court orders performance (real estate, unique goods) |
| **Rescission** | Contract voided, parties restored to prior position |

### UCC Article 2 vs. Common Law
| Issue | Common Law | UCC Article 2 |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| Governs | Services, real estate | Sale of goods |
| Acceptance | Mirror image | Battle of the forms (§2-207) |
| Modification | Needs consideration | No consideration needed |
| Output/Requirements | Not recognized | Recognized |

## Unit 4: Business Organizations

### Comparison
| Form | Liability | Taxation | Management |
|------|-----------|----------|------------|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Unlimited personal | Pass-through | Owner |
| **Partnership (GP)** | Unlimited personal | Pass-through | Partners |
| **Limited Partnership** | LP: limited; GP: unlimited | Pass-through | General partners |
| **LLC** | Limited | Flexible (pass-through default) | Members/managers |
| **C-Corporation** | Limited | Double (corp + shareholder) | Board of directors |
| **S-Corporation** | Limited | Pass-through | Board of directors |

### Corporate Law Basics
- **Piercing the corporate veil**: Courts hold shareholders personally liable when LLC/corp misused
  - Conditions: commingling funds, failure to follow formalities, fraud
- **Fiduciary duties**: Directors owe duty of care + loyalty
- **Business Judgment Rule**: Courts defer to board decisions made in good faith
