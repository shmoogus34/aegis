# CTZN-110 Notes — Social Entrepreneurship

## What is Social Entrepreneurship?
- Applying entrepreneurial principles to create **social value**, not just profit
- J. Gregory Dees (1998): Social entrepreneurs are "change agents for society"
- Key attributes: Mission-driven, innovative, resourceful, accountable
- Different from CSR: Social entrepreneurs build organizations around the mission

## Business Models for Social Impact

### Nonprofit / NGO
- Revenue: Grants, donations, government contracts
- Governance: Board of directors, tax-exempt (501(c)(3) in U.S.)
- Challenge: Donor dependency, sustainability concerns
- Example: Feeding America, Doctors Without Borders

### B-Corporation (Benefit Corporation)
- For-profit but legally required to consider social/environmental impact
- B-Lab certification: third-party verified against B Impact Assessment
- Example: Patagonia, Ben & Jerry's, Warby Parker

### Social Enterprise / Hybrid
- Earns revenue through market activities AND pursues social mission
- Example: TOMS (one-for-one model), Grameen Bank (microfinance)

### Impact Investing / ESG
- Investors intentionally target social/environmental returns alongside financial
- ESG: Environmental, Social, Governance criteria for investment screening
- Growing: >$35 trillion AUM in ESG globally (2021)

## Design Thinking for Social Problems

### 5 Stages
1. **Empathize**: Deep user research; interviews, observation, immersion
2. **Define**: Synthesize insights into a problem statement (HMW: How Might We...?)
3. **Ideate**: Divergent thinking; brainstorm without judgment (quantity → quality)
4. **Prototype**: Build quick, cheap models to test assumptions
5. **Test**: Get feedback from real users; iterate rapidly

### Key Mindsets
- Radical collaboration — diverse teams
- Bias toward action — make something
- Human-centered — empathy first
- Embrace ambiguity — no perfect solutions

## Theory of Change

### Logic Model Components
```
Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact
```
- **Inputs**: Resources (money, people, time)
- **Activities**: What the org does
- **Outputs**: Direct products of activities (# served)
- **Outcomes**: Changes in behavior/conditions (% employed)
- **Impact**: Long-term systemic change (reduced poverty rate)

### Social Return on Investment (SROI)
- SROI = Net Present Value of Social Outcomes / Total Investment
- Assigns monetary value to social outcomes
- Criticized for difficulty of measurement, potential for manipulation

## Global Citizenship

### Martha Nussbaum's Cosmopolitanism
- All human beings have equal moral worth regardless of nationality
- Obligations extend beyond national borders
- "World citizen" identity alongside local identities

### Oxfam's Global Citizenship Framework
- **Knowledge & Understanding**: Global issues, power structures
- **Skills**: Critical thinking, advocacy, cooperation
- **Values & Attitudes**: Commitment to equity, sustainability
- **Action**: Personal and collective change

## Case Studies

### Grameen Bank (Muhammad Yunus)
- Microfinance: Small loans to poor women in Bangladesh
- Group lending model: peer accountability
- >9 million borrowers; 97% repayment rate
- Nobel Peace Prize 2006
- Critique: high interest rates, borrower dependency

### TOMS Shoes
- "One-for-one" model: Buy one → Give one
- Critique: "Slacktivism," undermines local markets
- Evolution: shifted to 1/3 of profits to grassroots organizations

### Khan Academy
- Free education for everyone, everywhere
- Revenue: Donations (Gates Foundation, Google)
- Impact: 135M+ learners, 50+ languages
