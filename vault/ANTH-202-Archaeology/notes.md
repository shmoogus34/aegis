# ANTH-202 Lecture Notes

## Module 1: Foundations of Archaeology

### What is Archaeology?
- Sub-field of **anthropology** (in American tradition) that studies past human cultures through material remains
- Four-field approach: Cultural Anthropology, Biological Anthropology, Linguistic Anthropology, **Archaeology**
- Archaeologists study **artifacts**, **ecofacts**, and **features**

### Key Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Artifact** | Portable object made/used by humans (pottery, tools, coins) |
| **Ecofact** | Biological remains not directly made by humans (seeds, bones, pollen) |
| **Feature** | Non-portable evidence of human activity (hearths, post holes, refuse pits) |
| **Context** | The position of an artifact in time and space — CRITICAL for interpretation |
| **Provenience** | Precise 3D location (x, y, z coordinates) of an artifact |
| **Association** | Items found together in the same context |
| **Matrix** | Soil or sediment surrounding artifacts |

### Processual vs. Post-Processual Archaeology
**Processual (New Archaeology, 1960s-70s):**
- Lewis Binford
- Archaeology = science; seek universal laws of human behavior
- "Systemic context" vs. "archaeological context"
- Cultural ecology — environment shapes culture

**Post-Processual (1980s+):**
- Ian Hodder
- Meaning matters; interpret symbols and agency
- Critique of positivism; multiple valid interpretations
- "Archaeology is a political act"

## Module 2: Site Formation & Preservation

### Site Formation Processes
**Cultural Formation Processes (C-transforms):**
- Primary refuse: discarded where used
- Secondary refuse: moved to dump/midden
- De facto refuse: items left when a site is abandoned
- Caching: intentional storage

**Natural Formation Processes (N-transforms):**
- **Alluviation**: deposition by water
- **Colluviation**: slope wash
- **Bioturbation**: animal/plant disturbance (rodents, tree roots)
- **Cryoturbation**: freeze-thaw cycles

### Stratigraphy
- **Law of Superposition**: Older deposits are beneath younger ones
- **Harris Matrix**: diagram showing stratigraphic relationships
- **Terminus post quem (TPQ)**: earliest possible date for a deposit
- **Terminus ante quem (TAQ)**: latest possible date for a deposit

### Site Preservation
- Best preservation: anaerobic (waterlogged, frozen, arid) environments
- Organic materials decay rapidly in oxygenated, acidic soils
- **Cultural Resource Management (CRM)**: protecting sites from development
- **Section 106 of NHPA**: federal requirement to consult on historic properties before project approval

## Module 3: Survey and Excavation Methods

### Archaeological Survey
**Pedestrian Survey:**
- Systematic transects across landscape
- Shovel test pits (STPs): 50cm sq., spaced 15-30m apart
- Record artifacts, features, soil changes

**Remote Sensing:**
- LiDAR: strips vegetation to reveal earthworks
- Ground-penetrating radar (GPR): subsurface anomalies
- Magnetometry: detect burned features, hearths
- Aerial photography: crop/soil marks

### Excavation Techniques
**Grid System:**
- Datum point (fixed reference)
- Grid squares (1×1m or 2×2m)
- Excavate by **natural** or **arbitrary** levels

**Recording Methods:**
- Harris Matrix for stratigraphy
- Scaled plans and profiles (1:10, 1:20)
- Photo documentation (with scale, north arrow)
- Field notes and bag/lot numbers

**Screening:**
- 1/4 inch mesh: standard
- 1/8 inch mesh: faunal/botanical recovery
- Flotation: water separation of botanical remains

## Module 4: Dating Methods

### Relative Dating
| Method | Principle |
|--------|----------|
| **Stratigraphy** | Superposition — lower = older |
| **Typology** | Artifact styles change over time (seriation) |
| **Cross-dating** | Known-age objects in unknown context |
| **Fluorine/Nitrogen/Uranium** | Bone chemical changes over time |

### Absolute Dating
| Method | Material | Range | Precision |
|--------|----------|-------|-----------|
| **Radiocarbon (¹⁴C)** | Organic | 50,000 BP | ±40-80 yr |
| **Dendrochronology** | Wood | ~11,000 yr | ±1 yr |
| **Potassium-Argon** | Volcanic rock | 100k–4.5B yr | ±5% |
| **Thermoluminescence** | Ceramics, burned flint | 100k yr | ±10-15% |
| **Optically Stimulated Luminescence** | Sediment | 200k yr | ±5-10% |
| **Archaeomagnetism** | Burned clay | 10k yr | site-specific |

### Radiocarbon Dating Details
- ¹⁴C produced in atmosphere, absorbed by living organisms
- At death: ¹⁴C decays at known rate (half-life = 5,730 years)
- Results given as **BP (Before Present)** — present = 1950
- Must **calibrate** with IntCal database (atmospheric ¹⁴C fluctuates)
- REPORT as **calibrated BP** or **cal. BCE/CE**

## Module 5: Material Culture Analysis

### Ceramics
- **Attribute analysis**: form, decoration, paste, firing technique
- **Typology**: group types by shared attributes
- **Ware**: pottery tradition (e.g., Woodland, Mississippian)
- Construction: coil, paddle-and-anvil, wheel-thrown
- Firing: open firing, kiln (oxidizing vs. reducing atmosphere)

### Lithics (Stone Tools)
- **Flint knapping**: subtractive reduction technique
- **Conchoidal fracture**: predictable break pattern in fine-grained stone
- Types: biface, uniface, blade, core, flake
- Raw material sourcing: obsidian hydration, PIXE analysis
- **Debitage**: manufacturing waste from flint knapping

### Faunal Analysis (Zooarchaeology)
- Identifies species, age at death, season of occupation
- **NISP**: Number of Identified Specimens
- **MNI**: Minimum Number of Individuals
- Butchery marks → subsistence strategies
- Identifies hunting, herding, fishing, trade

### Botanical Analysis (Archaeobotany)
- **Flotation**: recovers carbonized seeds, wood charcoal
- Identifies diet, plant cultivation, forest use
- **Palynology (pollen analysis)**: past vegetation/climate reconstruction
- Phytoliths: plant silica bodies preserved in soil
