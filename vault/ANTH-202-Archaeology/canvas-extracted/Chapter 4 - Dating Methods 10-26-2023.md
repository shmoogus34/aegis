Typology
• Typology is defined as the systematic
organization of the artifacts into types on
the basis of shared attributes.
• It is a form of relative dating.

-- 6 of 126 --

History of Typology
• Mastered by 19th Century Swedish
archaeologist Oscar Montelius
– Used to organize European Bronze Age tool and
weapon forms
– Confirmed by stratigraphic excavations
– Showed that areas that border other areas tend to
share some design traits

-- 7 of 126 --

Principles of Typology #1
• Artifacts can be classified by style
• Products made in a specific region or time
usually have similar designs, making them
easier to be identified and grouped

Julian Calendar
• Julian Calendar
– Developed by Julius Ceaser in 46 B.C.
• Actually it was Sosigenes of Alexandria
who developed it based upon the work of
Aristarchus (239 B.C.)
– 3 years of 365 days each
– 4th year of 366 days
– The year began on January 1 to be in
sync with the Roman civil calendar.
– Followed imperfectly until 4 A.D.
• Leap Year?
• Corrected by 4 A.D.

-- 41 of 126 --

Julian Calendar
• Julian Calendar became common dating
method throughout the Roman Empire
• Anno Domini (A.D.) devised in 527 A.D.
– Anno Domini Nostri Iesu Christi
– Roman Abbott Dionysius Exiguus
• Popularized by our friend The Venerable Bede
– 725 AD – On the Reckoning of Time
– Problem! Did not account for first 12 months of
Year 0

-- 42 of 126 --

Julian Calendar – Problem!
• Julian Calendar Year = 365.25 days
– 1 additional day each 4th year
– 11.23 minutes longer than the Solar Year
• Every 131 years the calendar is out of sync
with the Equinoxes and Solstices by 1
additional day

-- 43 of 126 --

Gregorian Calendar
• The Council of Nicea in 325A.D.
established that Easter would be
held on the first Sunday after the
first full moon occurring on or
after the vernal equinox.
• Inaccuracy of the Julian Calendar
made it difficult for the Roman
Catholic Church to determine the
date of Easter.

-- 44 of 126 --

Gregorian Calendar
• Pope Paul III (1534-1549) sought
solutions from:
– Jesuit Priest Christopher Clavius
among others
• Gregory XIII becomes pope
(1572-1585) and decides to
resolve the problem

-- 45 of 126 --

Gregorian Calendar
• 1582 - Gregory XIII selected Clavius’s solution and
issues a Papal Bull that established the Gregorian
Calendar
• 3 years of 365.25 days
• 4th year of 366 days
– A year is a Leap Year if either
• The year is evenly divisible by 4;
• If the year can be evenly divided by 100, it is NOT a
leap year, unless;
– The year is also evenly divisible by 400. Then it is a leap
year.
• Ex: 1600 and 2000 are Leap Years; 1700, 1800, 1900
and 2100 are not.

-- 46 of 126 --

Gregorian Calendar
• In 1582, 10 days are omitted from the calendar
to bring it back into sync with the Solar Year
• Thursday, October 4, 1582 was followed by
Friday, October 15, 1582
• Catholic Countries of Portugal, Italy, Spain,
Poland, France and Luxembourg adopted it
immediately.

– Julian Calendar had drifted off by 1 more day by then
– Wednesday, September 2, 1752 was followed by
Thursday, September 14, 1752
– O.S. “Old Style” and N.S. “New Style”

Hard Water Effect
▪ Arises when the material to be dated synthesizes its
skeleton under water and uses biocarbonate derived
from old, inert sources
▪ Causes ages to be over-assessed
▪ If the sample consists of tree wood, it is assumed that
no hard water error exists

▪ caused both by the delay in exchange rates between
atmospheric CO2 and ocean bicarbonate
