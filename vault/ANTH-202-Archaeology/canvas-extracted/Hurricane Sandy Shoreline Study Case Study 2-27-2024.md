Weights of Evidence Technique
(WoE)
1. Identify “Training Points”
1. Known archaeological sites in seven counties
2. Withhold a fraction of these from the study area
to test the model later
3. Prepare “Evidence Layers” as rasters
1. Possible cultural correlates
2. Examples: elevation; soil; proximity to water
4. Calculate weights for each evidence layer
for each 10m x 10m area in the counties and sum
them.
5. Produce a “heat map” of the most likely areas to
find sites.

-- 17 of 47 --

Training Point
Selection
Site polygons in the DHR files:
Coded and Selected for “Native
American” only occupation.
Converted to points (centroids of
the DHR polygons)
In the study area, 50% of points
are randomly withheld from the
modeling.
All points in the control area are
used in modeling.

Elevation

-- 20 of 47 --

Elevation Results
1. Elevations up to 32 m AMSL are slightly
correlated with the presence of sites.
2. Elevations above 32 m AMSL are strongly
correlated with the absence of sites
3. These weights are then used in the Weights
of Evidence Model

-- 21 of 47 --

Aspect
(the compass
direction that sloping
land faces)
Aspect is derived from
the digital elevation
model, as a numeric
compass bearing.
Compass bearings are
then coded to one of
the eight compass
points.

-- 22 of 47 --

Aspect Results
1. Land which faces South or Southwest is correlated with the
presence of sites
2. Other compass directions are not significantly correlated with the
presence or absence of sites.
3. Land which is flat is negatively correlated with the presence of
sites

-- 23 of 47 --

Slope
Slope of the land is
derived from the
digital elevation
model.
Here it is shown in
degrees, not
percent rise-to-run.

-- 24 of 47 --

Slope Results
1. Slope was analyzed with a cumulative ascending
technique, properly assuming that lower slopes would
correlate with sites and that steep slopes would not, and
that a break-point could be determined for some
particular value of slope.
2. Analyzing slope this way turned out not to be statistically
significant at the model confidence level of 97.5%
3. This may be due to other factors, such as proximity to
water, taking precedent over slope in site selection by
Native Americans.
4. Slope was NOT USED as an input to the Weights of
Evidence Model

-- 25 of 47 --

Soil
Soil data was extracted
from the Soil Survey
Geographic Database
(SSURGO) of the
Natural Resources
Conservation Service
of the U.S. Department
of Agriculture

-- 26 of 47 --

Soil Results
1. Of 128 soils types in the seven county test area, 26 were found to
be statistically significant for the WoE model
2. Of those 26 soil types,
1. 7 were negatively correlated with sites, and
2. 19 were positively correlated with sites
3. The soils were used as an input to the WoE Model

-- 27 of 47 --

Clay
Content
1. The SSURGO soils database has the capacity to derive
soils classified by their clay content.
2. Native American populations might have chosen to
locate near clay sources, for pottery production.
3. We prepared a dataset with percent clay content, and
analyzed it using both ascending and descending
cumulative percent, but without achieving a statistically
significant result either way.

-- 28 of 47 --

Corn
Productivity
1. The SSSURGO soils dataset has the capacity to derive soils known for their
corn productivity (bushels per acre) assuming no irrigation.
2. We analyzed this data using descending cumulative weights, but did not
achieve a statistically significant result.

-- 29 of 47 --

Geology
The Geology of
Virginia shapefile
dataset produced 13
geological types across
the seven county study
area.
These were converted
to a categorical raster
for analysis.

-- 30 of 47 --

Geology Results
1. A categorical weights analysis showed that six
geological types, plus water, were significant for the
analysis of site location. Tc, Qsh, Qtpl, QTw, and Qtu.
2. Seven other types were not statistically significant. Tm,
Tb2, sb, al, m, Qc, Qtl
3. Of the statistically significant geology types, three were
positively correlated with sites (Tc, Qsh, and Qtu), and
three were negatively correlated with sites (Qtlp, Qts,
and QTw)
4. These weights were used as input to the Weights of
Evidence Model

-- 33 of 47 --

Proximity to Water results
1. A cumulative-ascending analysis was performed, assuming that
at some point, distance to water becomes too great for a site
location to be desirable.
2. The analysis showed that distances up to 400 m from water were
positively correlated with sites.
3. Analysis also showed that distances above 400 m were negatively
correlated with sites.

3. Soil type
4. Geological type
5. Distance from water
2. The output of the model is a post-probability
raster containing, for each 10m x 10m square, the
likelihood of finding a site
