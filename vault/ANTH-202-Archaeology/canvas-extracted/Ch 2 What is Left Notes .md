THE IMPORTANCE OF CONTEXT
CONTEXT (1)
•Crucial to understanding human activity on a site
•Consists of:
•Matrix – the material in which a find is located
•Provenience – 3-Dimensional location of the find in
the matrix
•Association – relationship between one find and the
others that may be around it
CONTEXT (2)
•Also a term that refers to discrete elements of the
archaeological record (features, structures, etc.)

-- 12 of 28 --

Primary Context
The situation where an archaeological element
being found where it was originally built, used,
deposited, or otherwise occurred.
Secondary Context
The situation where an archaeological element is
found in a place other than where it originally
occurred.
THE IMPORTANCE OF CONTEXT

Principle of Association
The concept that asserts that two artifacts found in close
proximity to one another are assumed to be associated
with the same time/period/event.

Principle of Superposition
The concept that asserts that the layers of the earth are
laid down one upon another. Lower levels are assumed to
be older than upper layers.

NATURAL TRANSFORMATIONS
Changes made to the archaeological record by natural
agents/processes that take place during and after site use
and abandonment
•Those that Destroy Sites
•Wind Erosion, Water Erosion, Gravity and Soil
Creep, Bacterial processes, Floral Turbation,
Faunal Turbation, Large Earth Movements –
earthquakes
•Those That Preserve Sites
•Arid
•Cold
•Waterlogged
