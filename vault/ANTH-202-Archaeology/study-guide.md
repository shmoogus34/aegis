# ANTH-202 Study Guide

## Key Concepts for Exams

### Definitions Flash Cards

**Q: What is the difference between an artifact and an ecofact?**
A: An artifact is a portable object made or modified by humans. An ecofact is biological/environmental material (bones, seeds, pollen) not made by humans but culturally relevant.

**Q: Define provenience.**
A: The precise three-dimensional location (x, y, z) of an artifact within a site. Loss of provenience = loss of context = loss of meaning.

**Q: What does the Law of Superposition state?**
A: In undisturbed deposits, older layers are below younger layers.

**Q: What is TPQ? TAQ?**
A: TPQ (terminus post quem) = earliest possible date for a layer; TAQ (terminus ante quem) = latest possible date. Mnemonic: TPQ = "after" the latest datable item inside.

**Q: What is cultural resource management (CRM)?**
A: Professional archaeology conducted to identify, evaluate, and protect historic/archaeological resources threatened by development, required by law (NHPA Section 106).

**Q: Explain processual vs. post-processual archaeology.**
A: Processual (Binford): seeks universal laws, scientific method, cultural ecology. Post-processual (Hodder): emphasizes meaning, symbolism, critique of objectivity; multiple interpretations valid.

### Dating Methods Quick Reference
| Method | Works On | Range |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Radiocarbon | Organic matter | <50,000 BP |
| Dendrochronology | Wood | ~11,000 yr |
| K-Ar | Volcanic ash | >100,000 yr |
| TL/OSL | Ceramics, sediment | <200,000 yr |
| Stratigraphy | Deposits | Relative only |

### Site Formation Mnemonic: "C-N transforms"
- **C** = Cultural transforms (human behavior: discard, reuse, curate)
- **N** = Natural transforms (environment: erosion, bioturbation, flooding)
