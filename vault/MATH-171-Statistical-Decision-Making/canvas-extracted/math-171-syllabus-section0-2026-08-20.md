Course Description: An elementary statistics course designed to give students a working knowledge of the ideas
and tools of practical statistics and their usefulness in problem solving and decision making. Topics include
graphical displays of data, measures of central tendency and variability, sampling distributions, and confidence
intervals and hypothesis testing for means and proportions. Additional topics may include elementary probability
concepts, correlation and regression, or Chi Square analysis. Special emphasis is placed upon the proper use and
interpretation of statistics in real life situations. 3 credits. G05; FQRC, WI.
Course Objectives: The course will give students a working knowledge of the ideas and tools of basic statistics.
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
• Explain various data collection methods for categorical and quantitative data and their roles in inference.
• Describe and analyze each type of data.
• Present and interpret data graphically (e.g., bar charts, histograms, and box plots).

• Compute and interpret measures of center and spread for quantitative data.

• Use the Central Limit Theorem in relation to the sampling distribution of the sample mean or sample

Quantitative Reasoning: Being a quantitative reasoning course in the Civitae Core Curriculum, the assignments in
this course will be designed to engage and instruct students in the process of quantitative reasoning. Specifically,
at the conclusion of the course, students will have repeatedly investigated matters related to strategic and dynamic
decision-making in cooperative and competitive situations by
1. formulating questions/issues using appropriate mathematical, algorithmic, and/or statistical terms, explain-
ing the decision process behind the choices made in their formulation.
2. using mathematical, algorithmic, and/or statistical methods to gather and/or analyze data.
3. determining the reasonableness of their answer and/or evaluating the explanations of data for reasonable-
ness.
4. interpreting the results of their mathematical, algorithmic, and/or statistical analysis, presenting the inter-
pretation in a context appropriate for a broader audience.
The quantitative reasoning process outlined will be applied to problems requiring statistical analysis, but this
same process can be called upon and applied whenever the student faces a problem that requires their quantitative
reasoning abilities.
Course Structure and Student Expectations: This is a lecture based course.Your attendance and engagement to

Writing Assignments: This course is part of the Civitae Core Curriculum and is writing-infused. Writing As-
signments are designed with the intention of helping you improve your writing and communication of ideas,
questions, and topics that involve quantitative reasoning.
Beyond just addressing problems that require statistical analysis, a portion of this class will be devoted to
communicating the results of statistical analysis in the context of the original problem to a broader audience.
To this end, the class will include a number of writing assignments. The length of the assignments and the
amount of time they require for completion will vary (some will be completed in class, some will require
additional time outside of class), but students will have ample time for questions. Specific instruction related
to audience, purpose, and context will be provided. In particular, emphasis will be placed on communicating
results in a way that allows the presentation of the results to establish context and stand independently on
its own merits without assuming that the reader has knowledge of (or access to) a motivating assignment.
Feedback will be provided on all writing assignments with the intent that it will be incorporated into future
writing assignments to improve the quality of presentation (and your grade!).
