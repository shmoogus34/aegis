Chapter 1
Individuals : The objects described by a set of data. (Note: ‘Individuals’ are not limited to people)
Variables: A variable is any characteristic of an individual. We use the term variable on account of the fact
that the characteristic can vary from individual to individual.
We typically split our variables into two types:
1. Categorical Variables: A categorical variable places in an individual into one of several groups or categories
2. Quantitative Variables: A quantitative variable takes numerical values for which arithmetic operations such as
adding and averaging make sense. Typically, there is a unit of measurement (kg, sec, lbs, inches, feet, etc.)
attached.
Ex. 1. (Cat. vs. Quant.) A Consumer Reports article about 25 tablets lists each tablet’s manufacturer, cost,
battery life (in hours), operating system (iOS/Android/RIM), and overall performance score (0-100). Identify all
variables being used to describe the tablets and identify each as a categorical or quantitative variable.
Ex. 2. Suppose you want to study Longwood University students. Provide examples of 5 quantitative variables
and 5 categorical variables that you might be interested in gathering data about.
Ex. 3. (Bar Charts) A survey asks 2252 people have been asked what type of cell phone they have. 458 an
Android, 437 have an iPhone, 141 have a Blackberry, 924 have a standard cellphone, and 292 indicated that the do
not have a cell phone.
(a) What is the variable of interest? Is it categorical or quantitative?
(b) What proportion of individuals from the survey have an Android? What proportion have in iPhone?
(c) Construct both a frequency bar graph (to a reasonable scale) and a relative frequency bar graph that summarizes
the data.
