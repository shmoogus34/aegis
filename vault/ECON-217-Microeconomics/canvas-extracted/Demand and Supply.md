One big assumption:
Ceteris Paribus  Nothing else is changing or Everything else is held constant
Under this assumption, the relationship b/w price and quantity demanded/supplied are measured. And any changes in price will lead to a movement 
along
 the demand or supply curve. 
What happens when we allow other things (war, income, price of substitutes, preference, cost of materials, etc.) to change?
When other things are changing (i.e., we do not assume Ceteris Paribus)  then the entire demand or supply curve will shift. 
Mamun’s Rule of Demand and Supply
When the Prices of the goods &amp; services change  Then we move along the demand or supply curve  This is called the change in Quantity Demanded or Supplied
When anything other than the price of the goods &amp; services changes  Then we have a shift of the demand or supply curves  This is called a change in Demand or Supply.
If the anything other than the price is good or 
positive
  Then the Demand or Supply curves will shift to Right 
If the anything other than the price is bad or 
negative
  then the Demand or Supply curves will shift to Left
