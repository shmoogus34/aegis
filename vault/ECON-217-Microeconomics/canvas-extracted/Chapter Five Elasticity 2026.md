Chapter Five: Elasticity
Elasticity: A measure of responsiveness 
If a variable X changes by 1% then the percentage change in the Y variable is called the X elasticity of Y or in other words, the response of Y to the change in X. 
For 
examples

Price Elasticity of Demand: 1% change in Gasoline price  % change in Gasoline Quantity Demanded
Price Elasticity of Supply: 1% change in wholesale price of orange juice  % change in orange juice supplied by the farmers. 
Income Elasticity of Demand: 1% change in income  % change in demand for vacations
Cross Price Elasticity of Demand: 1% change in price of apples  % change in demand for orange

Definitions:
Elastic: When the response to a change in QD is greater than the percentage change in price. ED &gt; -1
Unit Elastic: When the response to a change in QD is exactly equal to the percentage change in Price.  ED = -1
Inelastic: When the response to a change in QD is smaller than the percentage change in price. ED &lt; -1

Relationship between Elasticity and Revenue:
When demand is inelastic  Raising price can increase revenue/sales
When demand is elastic   Raising price will decrease revenue/sales
The opposite is also true when we lower prices. 
Definitions:
Total Revenue = P x Q
Total Cost = Avg. Cost x Q
Profit = Total Revenue – Total Cost
Using Elasticity to predict the quantity of demand
ED=% ∆ in QD% ∆ in P
% ∆ in QD% ∆ in P=ED
% ∆ in QD=ED×% ∆ in P

% ∆ in QD=ED×% ∆ in P
% ∆ in QD=-1.5×10%=-15%
