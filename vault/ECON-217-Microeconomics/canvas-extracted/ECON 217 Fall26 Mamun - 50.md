In this introductory course, students learn to think like an economist and apply
microeconomic principles to the study of how individuals and societies make
decisions in the face of scarcity. By the end of the course, students will be able
to combine abstract concepts with formal analytical tools, such as supply and
demand, in order to understand how markets work, how individuals and firms
make decisions, and how the decisions impact real market outcomes.
Applications to current public policy issues are highlighted throughout the
course, for example, issues surrounding minimum wage laws and trade policy.
Business applications include understanding profit maximization, consumer
price sensitivity, and competitive and monopolistic industries. 3 credits.

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:
• Explain opportunity costs and scarcity and apply these concepts to decision-making.
• Illustrate the concept of “think on the margin” and then apply this marginal analysis to decision-
making in economic models.
• Define and contrast absolute and comparative advantage and argue why all countries benefit from
trade.
• Use the model of supply and demand to illustrate changes in equilibrium price and quantity.
• Identify consumer and producer surplus and how changes in supply or demand impact surplus.
• Calculate, interpret, and apply price elasticity of demand to pricing decisions.

Papers and Presentations (60%):
There will be two papers and presentations during the semester and at the end of this course.
The first two papers will carry 15% each. The last paper and in-person presentation will
carry 30% of your grade.
The topic of the paper must be economics-related. We will have a deadline for you to pre-
select a topic for each paper. You are allowed to use any AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude) to write
the papers. The first two presentations need to be recorded and uploaded to Canvas. You are
allowed some latitude in terms of recorded presentations. For example, you can make it
different by using AI to use your voice on pictures/videos, etc. The final presentation will be
in person. Business casual attire and a PowerPoint will be needed.
I will also be relying on an honor code for my class. I believe that my students are
responsible human beings. So, I would expect a higher standard from you. I also believe in
Tao Te Ching’s quote, “When the student is ready the teacher will appear. When the student
is truly ready... The teacher will Disappear.” You are in my course for learning, and I am
here to teach. And it is my sincere hope that you will not need me anymore by the end of the
semester.
Homework (20%):
The homework will be posted on Canvas. The HWs are based on article/s from WSJ, NYT
or other sourced materials. They will require you to read the article/s and then answer 2 – 5
questions. These HWs are not timed, and you will be able to access and do the homework
multiple times on Canvas. If the HW requires you to draw graphs, then you will draw them
by hand and then take pictures of the drawing OR draw them using an AI tool. You can then
either upload the pictures on Canvas or copy-paste them into the Word file. You can use any
AI tool to do research for your HWs. But do not just get the answers from AI and paste
them. First of all, the answers can be incorrect, and most importantly, you will not learn
anything by doing so.
All homework answers must be submitted through Canvas. Students must complete
homework by the due date and time. Only under extraordinary circumstances (such as
hospitalization), you may appeal for a homework extension. Missed homework will not be
counted toward your grade.
You can use the textbook/your notes for the homework; however, you are expected to work
on and submit all assignments by yourself only. I encourage you to study in groups in
preparation for the homework and exams. But any assignments you complete must be done
independently and must be solely your own work. Unauthorized collaboration is considered
cheating and is a violation of the Honor Code.
Once your assignments have been graded, you will be able to view the grades and feedback
on Canvas. At the end of the semester, I will drop your lowest homework grade.
