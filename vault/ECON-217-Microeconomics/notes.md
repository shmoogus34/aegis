# ECON-217 Lecture Notes

## Chapter 1: Supply and Demand

### Demand
- **Law of Demand**: Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓ (ceteris paribus)
- Demand curve: downward-sloping in P-Q space
- **Shifters of Demand** (move entire curve):
  - Income (normal vs. inferior goods)
  - Price of related goods (substitutes vs. complements)
  - Tastes and preferences
  - Number of buyers
  - Expectations of future price

### Supply
- **Law of Supply**: Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑
- Supply curve: upward-sloping
- **Shifters of Supply**:
  - Input prices (wages, raw materials)
  - Technology
  - Number of sellers
  - Government policies (taxes, subsidies)
  - Expectations

### Market Equilibrium
- Equilibrium: Qd = Qs at price P*
- **Surplus** (excess supply): P > P* → price falls
- **Shortage** (excess demand): P < P* → price rises
- **Price Ceiling**: Max price below equilibrium → shortage (e.g., rent control)
- **Price Floor**: Min price above equilibrium → surplus (e.g., minimum wage)

## Chapter 2: Elasticity

### Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
$$PED = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{\Delta Q / Q}{\Delta P / P}$$

| |PED| | Classification | Revenue effect of price ↑ |
|----|----------------|---------------------------|
| > 1 | **Elastic** | TR falls |
| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | TR unchanged |
| < 1 | **Inelastic** | TR rises |
| = 0 | **Perfectly inelastic** | TR rises |
| = ∞ | **Perfectly elastic** | TR → 0 |

**Determinants of PED:**
- Availability of substitutes: more substitutes → more elastic
- Necessity vs. luxury: necessities → inelastic
- Time horizon: long run → more elastic
- Budget share: larger share → more elastic

### Income Elasticity (YED)
$$YED = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta Y}$$
- YED > 0: **Normal good** (demand rises with income)
- YED < 0: **Inferior good** (demand falls with income)
- YED > 1: **Luxury good**

### Cross-Price Elasticity (XED)
$$XED = \frac{\% \Delta Q_{A}}{\% \Delta P_{B}}$$
- XED > 0: **Substitutes** (Pepsi & Coke)
- XED < 0: **Complements** (cars & gas)

### Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)
$$PES = \frac{\% \Delta Q_s}{\% \Delta P}$$
- Short run: more inelastic (can't adjust quickly)
- Long run: more elastic (firms can enter/exit)

## Chapter 3: Consumer Theory

### Utility
- **Utility**: Measure of satisfaction (ordinal, not cardinal in modern theory)
- **Total Utility (TU)**: Total satisfaction from consuming Q units
- **Marginal Utility (MU)**: Additional utility from one more unit
- **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility**: MU falls as Q increases

### Utility Maximization
Consumer equilibrium (interior solution):
$$\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y}$$

"Bang per buck" must be equal across all goods.

### Budget Constraint
$$P_x \cdot X + P_y \cdot Y = I$$
- Slope = $-P_x / P_y$
- Intercepts: $I/P_x$ (X-axis), $I/P_y$ (Y-axis)

### Indifference Curves
- Downward-sloping, convex to origin
- **MRS** (Marginal Rate of Substitution) = $MU_x / MU_y$ = slope at a point
- Optimum: where IC is tangent to budget line
  - MRS = $P_x/P_y$

### Income and Substitution Effects (Price Change)
- **Substitution effect**: Good is now relatively cheaper → buy more (always opposes price change)
- **Income effect**: Real income changed → buy more (normal) or less (inferior)
- For **Giffen goods**: Income effect > substitution effect; demand curve slopes upward (extremely rare)

## Chapter 4: Production Theory

### Production Function
$$Q = f(K, L)$$
- **Short run**: At least one input fixed (usually capital K)
- **Long run**: All inputs variable

### Short-Run Costs
| Cost | Formula | Behavior |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Fixed Cost (FC)** | Constant | Horizontal |
| **Variable Cost (VC)** | Changes with Q | Rising |
| **Total Cost (TC)** | FC + VC | Rising |
| **MC** | ΔTC/ΔQ | U-shaped |
| **AFC** | FC/Q | Falling |
| **AVC** | VC/Q | U-shaped |
| **ATC** | TC/Q = AFC+AVC | U-shaped |

**Key relationships:**
- MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimums
- When MC < AVC: AVC falling
- When MC > AVC: AVC rising

### Long-Run Costs & Returns to Scale
- **Economies of scale**: LRATC falls as output ↑
- **Diseconomies of scale**: LRATC rises as output ↑
- **Constant returns to scale**: LRATC flat

## Chapter 5: Market Structures

### Perfect Competition
- Many buyers and sellers; identical products; free entry/exit; perfect info
- **Price taker**: P = MR = AR for firms
- **Profit max**: MR = MC, i.e., P = MC
- **Short-run shut-down rule**: Shut down if P < AVC
- **Long-run**: P = min ATC (zero economic profit)

### Monopoly
- Single seller; barriers to entry; price maker
- MR < P (must lower price to sell more)
- Profit max: MR = MC; charge P from demand curve
- **Deadweight loss (DWL)**: Social inefficiency from monopoly restriction
- **Price discrimination**: Charge different prices to different buyers
  - 1st degree: charge each consumer their WTP
  - 2nd degree: quantity discounts
  - 3rd degree: segment markets (students, seniors)

### Oligopoly
- Few firms; strategic interdependence; high barriers to entry
- **Game theory**: payoff matrices, dominant strategies
- **Nash Equilibrium**: No player benefits by unilaterally deviating
- **Prisoners' Dilemma**: Individual rationality → collective irrationality
- **Cournot**: Compete on quantity
- **Bertrand**: Compete on price → competitive outcome

## Chapter 6: Market Failures

### Externalities
- **Negative externality**: Social cost > private cost (e.g., pollution)
  - MSC = MPC + External Cost
  - Overproduction in free market
  - Remedy: Pigouvian tax, cap-and-trade
- **Positive externality**: Social benefit > private benefit (e.g., education)
  - MSB = MPB + External Benefit
  - Underproduction in free market
  - Remedy: Subsidy

### Public Goods
- **Non-rival**: One person's use doesn't reduce availability
- **Non-excludable**: Can't prevent non-payers from using
- **Free-rider problem** → underprovision by private market
- Examples: national defense, lighthouses, fireworks displays

### Information Asymmetry
- **Adverse selection**: Before transaction (e.g., lemons market — Akerlof)
- **Moral hazard**: After transaction (e.g., insurance)
- **Signaling**: Costly signal credibly conveys information (education — Spence)
